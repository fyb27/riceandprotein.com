Diet Guide

OMAD (One Meal A Day)

You eat everything in one single meal per day. Nothing else.

What is it?

OMAD is the most extreme form of intermittent fasting. You eat one large meal a day and fast for the remaining 23 hours. Water, black coffee, and plain tea are allowed during the fasting period.

The one meal should contain your full daily calorie target. This is usually between 1500 and 2500 calories in a single sitting, depending on your size and goal.

There are no rules about which foods to eat. You just eat them all at once.

How does it actually work?

Think of it like charging your phone once a day. You plug in, charge fully, and then unplug. The phone runs on that single charge for the next 23 hours. Your body works the same way on OMAD. You fuel up completely in one meal, then your body runs on that fuel and stored body fat for the rest of the day. The long fasting window keeps insulin low and encourages fat burning.

What does a typical day look like?

Morning to late afternoon (fasting)
Water, black coffee, or plain tea only. No food until the meal window.
One meal at 6pm (1800 to 2200 kcal)
300g rice. 400g chicken breast or beef. 300g vegetables. 2 eggs. Fruit for dessert. Everything in one sitting.
After 7pm (fasting again)
Nothing until 6pm the next day.

Is this diet for you?

Good if you...
  • Already do 16:8 fasting and want to go further
  • Have a very busy schedule with no time for meals
  • Are experienced with fasting and hunger
Skip it if you...
  • Are new to dieting or fasting
  • Have blood sugar issues or diabetes
  • Train hard at the gym and need regular fuel

Pros and Cons

The Science

OMAD creates a very long fasting window, which reduces total calorie intake in many people by limiting the time available to eat. A 2019 study found that OMAD produced similar weight loss to a standard diet but was associated with higher hunger levels throughout the day. Some unfavorable changes to blood lipid levels were also noted. It works well for people who tolerate extended fasting, but it is not suitable for everyone and should be approached carefully by beginners.
