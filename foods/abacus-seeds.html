How Many Calories in Abacus Seeds?

A Hakka stir-fried dish of chewy yam-tapioca dough beads tossed with minced pork, dried shrimp, mushroom and black fungus, popular in Hakka communities across Malaysia.

520kcal / 1 plate
One plate of Abacus Seeds (around 320g) comes in at roughly 520 calories, with 22g protein, 65g carbs and 18g fat. The yam-tapioca dough beads carry most of the carb load, while the minced pork and cooking oil push the fat and protein up. Fair warning, these numbers are a ballpark estimate since hard nutritional data for this dish is basically nonexistent, but 500-plus calories for a full plate is a reasonable working figure.

The real talk

Abacus Seeds (算盘子, suan pan zi) is a Hakka dish you'll find at Hakka restaurants and some heritage kopitiam spots across Malaysia, especially in KL, Bentong and parts of Sabah. If you've never had it, the dough beads are made from yam and tapioca starch, then hand-rolled into little round shapes that look like abacus beads, boiled and stir-fried with minced pork, dried shrimp, shiitake mushrooms and black fungus. The texture is QQ and slightly sticky, think a chewier version of mochi but savory. At 520 calories a plate it's not a diet food, but it's also not a disaster. The yam dough beads alone account for 290 of those calories, and the minced pork adds another 145. To be straight with you though, this is a medium-confidence estimate because proper lab-tested nutrition data for Abacus Seeds is basically nonexistent. Take the number as a useful guide, not gospel.

What actually moves the number is how heavy-handed the wok is with oil and how big the portion is. A generous cook who doubles the oil can easily add 80 to 100 extra calories without you noticing. Portion size is the other big lever: a smaller serve of 200g instead of 320g would drop you closer to 325 calories. The minced pork contributes real protein but also fat, so if you could swap in lean chicken mince you'd save maybe 30 to 40 calories. Ask for less oil if you're ordering at a restaurant, most places will accommodate. The dried shrimp and mushrooms are practically free at only 40 calories for 30g, so don't stress about those.

How to eat it lighter

  • Ask for less oil at the wok. This dish traditionally uses a fair bit and even cutting it by half saves you around 40 to 60 calories.
  • Eat a smaller portion. 200g instead of 320g puts you around 325 calories and honestly still feels satisfying because the dough is dense and chewy.
  • Pair it with a clear soup or a plate of stir-fried sayur manis instead of ordering extra rice or another fried item.
  • Skip any extra sauce or gravy that some places pour over. That stuff adds calories fast and you can't easily account for it.
  • Share a plate between two people and bulk up with a simple vegetable dish on the side. You still get to enjoy the flavour without eating the full 520 calories yourself.

Common questions

How many calories in Abacus Seeds?
One plate of Abacus Seeds (roughly 320g) is around 520 calories. That breaks down to about 22g protein, 65g carbs and 18g fat. This is a solid estimate but hard to pin down precisely since the dish doesn't have much published nutritional data.
What exactly are Abacus Seeds?
Abacus Seeds are a Hakka dish made from yam and tapioca starch dough that's hand-shaped into little bead-like rounds, boiled until chewy, then stir-fried with minced pork, dried shrimp, mushrooms and black fungus. They taste savory and slightly earthy from the yam, with a QQ chewy bite. It's not pork-free, so non-halal.
Is Abacus Seeds healthy?
It's a balanced enough meal with decent protein from the pork and reasonable carbs from the yam dough, but 520 calories a plate means it's not a light option. The bigger issue is the oil used in stir-frying, which you can't always control. If you're counting calories, treat it as a proper meal rather than a snack, and maybe skip the sugary drink that usually comes with it.
