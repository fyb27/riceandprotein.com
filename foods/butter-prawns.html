How Many Calories in Butter Prawns?

Shell-on prawns deep-fried then wok-tossed in a rich butter, curry leaf and egg-floss sauce, which is as good as it sounds and as calorie-heavy as you'd expect.

580kcal / 1 restaurant portion (6-8 medium-large prawns, shell-on, with egg floss)
A restaurant portion of Butter Prawns (6-8 medium-large shell-on prawns with egg floss) comes in at around 580 calories, with 28g protein, 14g carbs and 44g fat. The prawns themselves are not the problem; it's the butter, the deep-frying oil and those crispy egg-yolk strands that stack the numbers fast. Note that this is a ballpark estimate because reliable lab data for this dish is thin, so treat it as a realistic guide rather than gospel.

The real talk

Butter Prawns is one of those dishes where the main ingredient is genuinely good for you and then the cooking method absolutely wrecks the macro profile. You get prawns, which are lean and high-protein, but they go into a deep fryer first, then get tossed in a wok with a generous amount of butter, evaporated milk, curry leaves, and egg-yolk floss fried in more butter. The egg floss alone contributes around 115 calories. The butter base adds another 130 calories. So out of 580 total calories, roughly 390 come from fat sources. If you've never had this dish, picture crispy golden prawns coated in a fragrant, slightly creamy sauce with delicate egg strands that melt on your tongue. It's genuinely one of the better things Malaysian Chinese cuisine has produced. Worth eating. Just not every day, lah.

The version you order matters a lot. A home-cooked pan-fried version with the same butter and egg floss but without deep-frying sits around 280 calories, basically half the restaurant number, because you skip the absorbed frying oil. The salted egg version pushes up to 640 calories because salted egg yolks replace or supplement the egg floss and those yolks are very fat-dense. Portion size is another big variable. A restaurant order is typically shared between two or three people alongside rice and vegetables, so your actual intake might be 180-200 calories per person if you split it properly. The calorie estimates here are best guesses since standardised data for this dish is genuinely hard to find, so treat the numbers as a solid ballpark rather than exact figures.

How to eat it lighter

  • Share it properly. Split one restaurant portion between two people and you're looking at roughly 290 calories each, which is perfectly manageable.
  • Order the pan-fried version when you can, or ask at hawker stalls. Skipping the deep-fry step cuts about 80 calories straight away.
  • Eat it with plain steamed rice, not fried rice. The dish already has enough fat going on, you don't need more from the rice.
  • Go easy on the sauce at the bottom of the plate. A lot of the butter and oil pools there. You can leave it and lose maybe 50-80 calories.
  • Skip the salted egg upgrade. The regular egg floss version saves you around 60 calories per portion and honestly tastes just as good.

Common questions

How many calories in Butter Prawns?
A standard restaurant portion of Butter Prawns, which is 6-8 medium-large shell-on prawns with egg floss sauce, contains around 580 calories. A home-cooked pan-fried version is closer to 280 calories, and the salted egg variant goes up to about 640 calories.
What exactly is Butter Prawns and what makes it so rich?
Butter Prawns is a Malaysian Chinese dish where shell-on prawns are deep-fried then wok-tossed in a sauce of butter, evaporated milk and curry leaves, finished with crispy egg-yolk floss. The combination of deep-frying oil absorbed by the prawns plus the butter sauce base is what drives the calorie count so high. The egg floss alone adds around 115 calories to the dish.
Is Butter Prawns a good source of protein or should I avoid it when dieting?
The prawns themselves are solid protein at around 28g per portion, which is decent. The problem is the fat coming in at 44g, mostly from butter and frying oil. It's not a dish to avoid entirely, but it's better treated as an occasional order and shared between people rather than a solo serving. Pair it with plain rice and vegetables and you've got a reasonable meal overall.
