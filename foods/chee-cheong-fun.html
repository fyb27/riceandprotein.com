How Many Calories in Chee Cheong Fun?

Silky steamed rice noodle rolls drenched in sweet sauce and fried shallots, one of the most popular morning dishes in KL and Ipoh.

350kcal / 1 plate (3-4 rolls, KL sweet sauce style)
A standard plate of Chee Cheong Fun (KL sweet sauce style, 3 to 4 rolls) comes in at around 350 calories for roughly 260g. Most of those calories are from the rice noodle rolls themselves (about 215 calories for 200g of plain rolls), with the sweet sauce adding another 55 calories on top. It sounds light, but that sauce plus the fried shallots and sesame seeds stack up faster than you'd expect.

The real talk

Okay so at 350 calories for a plate of Chee Cheong Fun, this is not bad on its face. The noodles are basically just rice and water, so the rolls themselves are 215 calories for 200g. The issue is the toppings. That sweet sauce (tim cheong, a sweet soy-based sauce) clocks in at 55 calories for 30g, and then the fried shallots and sesame seeds add another 65 calories for about 15g. That is 120 calories of pure coating before you even look at the noodles. Protein is only 8g, which means this is mostly a carb meal. Fair warning: these numbers are a ballpark estimate. Hard nutritional data for Malaysian hawker food is thin, so treat this as an educated guess, not a lab result.

What actually moves the maths here is the sauce and the style. KL sweet sauce style at 350 calories is the baseline. Switch to Ipoh or Penang style with curry gravy and wet prawn paste, and you are already at 420 calories because the gravy is fat-heavy. Add three or four pieces of fried yong tau foo on top and you are at 500 calories, but honestly at that point your protein goes up too (probably 18 to 20g), so it is a better trade-off if you are watching macros. The lighter move is to ask for less sauce. In KL most hawkers just drown the thing, so asking for light sauce on the side actually saves you close to 40 to 50 calories without killing the taste.

How to eat it lighter

  • Ask for light sauce or sauce on the side. That sweet tim cheong sauce is 55 calories for just 30g, and hawkers are generous with it.
  • Skip or reduce the fried shallots. They are the sneaky one. Sesame seeds plus fried shallots together clock in at 65 calories for a 15g sprinkle.
  • Order the plain 2-roll version first and see if you are actually still hungry before going for a third or fourth roll.
  • Choose the steamed yong tau foo version if you want more food. Steamed tofu and fish items add protein without nearly as much fat as the fried versions.
  • If the hawker has both KL and Penang style, go KL. Curry gravy and wet prawn paste (hae ko) in the Penang version add significant fat that pushes calories up to 420 per plate.

Common questions

How many calories in Chee Cheong Fun?
A standard KL-style plate with sweet sauce, chili, sesame, and fried shallots (3 to 4 rolls, about 260g) is around 350 calories. Go Ipoh or Penang style with curry gravy and prawn paste and you're looking at closer to 420 calories. Add yong tau foo toppings and it can hit 500 calories.
Is Chee Cheong Fun good for weight loss?
It can be, if you keep the toppings in check. The plain rice noodles themselves are not that high in calories. The damage comes from the sweet sauce (55 calories for just 30g) and the fried shallots. Ask for light sauce, skip the extra shallots, and it becomes a reasonably sensible meal.
Which style of Chee Cheong Fun has the most calories?
The yong tau foo version is the heaviest at around 500 calories, because you're adding fried tofu and fish paste items on top. Ipoh or Penang style with curry gravy comes in at about 420 calories. KL sweet sauce style at 350 calories is the lightest of the three main variants.
Berapa kalori dalam Chee Cheong Fun?
Chee Cheong Fun ada kira-kira 350 kalori untuk 1 pinggan (260g), dengan 8g protein, 58g karbohidrat dan 10g lemak. Angka ini anggaran untuk hidangan gerai biasa, jadi ia berbeza ikut kedai dan saiz hidangan.
