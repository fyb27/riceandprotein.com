How Many Calories in Chicken 65?

Boneless chicken bites marinated in spices and yogurt, battered, deep-fried until bright red, then tossed in a curry leaf and dried chili tempering.

460kcal / 1 plate (hawker/mamak portion, ~8-10 pieces)
A standard mamak or hawker plate of Chicken 65 (8 to 10 pieces, about 150g) will cost you around 460 calories, with 29g protein, 14g carbs and 30g fat. The chicken itself is not the villain here. It is the batter plus the oil absorbed during deep frying that pushes it into heavy territory, adding roughly 190 calories on top of what plain chicken would give you.

The real talk

Chicken 65 is a South Indian dish that landed in Malaysian mamak culture and never left. The concept is simple: boneless chicken marinated in yogurt and spices, coated in a cornstarch and rice flour batter, deep-fried till crispy, then tossed in a fragrant tempering of curry leaves, dried chili and whole spices. That bright reddish-orange colour is from the chili and food colouring, not some magic sauce. At 460 calories for a plate, it is on the heavier side for a lauk, and the fat content (30g) tells you exactly why. The batter coating absorbs oil during frying, and that absorbed oil alone accounts for about 115 of those calories. Protein is decent at 29g though, so if you are eating it as your main protein with plain rice it is not a disaster, just be honest with yourself about portion size. Fair warning: these numbers are a ballpark estimate because solid nutrition data for this specific dish is thin. Every mamak makes it differently, so treat 460 as a reasonable midpoint, not gospel.

What actually moves the number is how much tempering oil the cook uses at the end. A dry-tossed version (curry leaves only, minimal extra oil) can drop you to around 380 calories for the same plate. A saucy restaurant version with thick chili-yogurt sauce and heavy tempering can push you up to 530 calories or more. If you are ordering at a mamak, ask for less gravy or sauce if there is one. The other lever is portion, which is obvious but real: 8 pieces versus 10 pieces can be a 50 to 60 calorie swing depending on piece size. Pair it with plain rice and a vegetable rather than nasi kandar-style with three other lauk, and the meal stays manageable.

How to eat it lighter

  • Ask for the dry version, no extra sauce or gravy on top. That alone can save you 50 to 80 calories compared to the saucy restaurant style.
  • Eat it as your only protein for that meal. It gives you 29g of protein so you do not need to stack it with rendang and curry on the same plate.
  • Pair with plain steamed rice instead of nasi goreng or rice cooked in fat. Saves you 100 to 150 calories on the carb side.
  • Stick to 8 pieces and do not go back for more just because they are sitting there. A 10-piece versus 8-piece difference is roughly 60 to 75 calories.
  • Skip the teh tarik that usually comes with it at mamak. That drink alone adds 100 to 140 calories. Get plain water or teh o kosong instead.

Common questions

How many calories in Chicken 65?
A standard mamak or hawker plate of Chicken 65, around 8 to 10 pieces (150g), has approximately 460 calories. The deep-frying and oil absorption are the main reasons it is calorie-dense despite being a chicken dish.
What exactly is Chicken 65?
It is a South Indian dish of boneless chicken marinated in yogurt and spices, coated in a cornstarch and rice flour batter, then deep-fried and tossed in a tempering of curry leaves, dried chili and whole spices. It is a mamak staple in Malaysia, recognisable by its bright red colour.
Is Chicken 65 a good source of protein?
Yes, 29g of protein per plate is solid. The issue is the fat (30g), mostly from the battered deep-frying. If you are in a calorie deficit, it is not off-limits but you want to eat it as your main protein for that meal rather than one of several lauk on a loaded rice plate.
Berapa kalori dalam Chicken 65?
Chicken 65 ada kira-kira 460 kalori untuk 1 pinggan (150g), dengan 29g protein, 14g karbohidrat dan 30g lemak. Angka ini anggaran untuk hidangan gerai biasa, jadi ia berbeza ikut kedai dan saiz hidangan.
