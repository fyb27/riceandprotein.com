How Many Calories in Claypot Chicken Rice?

A classic Chinese hawker rice dish cooked in a clay pot with chicken thigh, lap cheong, salted fish, and dark soy, crispy at the bottom and smoky all the way through.

700kcal / 1 claypot (single serve)
Claypot Chicken Rice comes in at around 700 calories for a single-serve pot (roughly 380g), which is a solid, filling meal but not exactly light. You're getting 38g of protein, 70g of carbs, and 27g of fat. The chicken thigh, lap cheong, and salted fish all pull their weight here, calorie-wise and flavour-wise.

The real talk

Honestly, 700 calories for a full claypot is not terrible for a complete meal. You're getting 38g of protein and enough carbs to keep you going. The problem is that the lap cheong is doing a lot of damage quietly, 30g of Chinese sausage clocks in at 120 calories and is mostly fat, and most people don't even notice it's there because it just tastes like part of the rice. The salted fish adds flavour but not many calories, so that's fine. The real culprits are the sausage and the skin-on thigh, which together account for about 340 of your 700 calories. Fair warning though: these numbers are a ballpark estimate, hard data on claypot rice is thin and every hawker cooks it differently, so treat this as a reasonable guide, not a lab result.

What changes the maths a lot is what you order and how much rice they pack in. A plain chicken claypot without sausage or salted fish drops to around 530 calories, which is actually a pretty decent calorie-to-fullness ratio for a rice meal. If you're eating at a restaurant and sharing a bigger pot, your half could easily hit 750 calories depending on how generous the cook is with oil and sausage. The sauce mix, dark soy, oyster sauce, and sesame oil adds about 60 calories on its own, and some places are heavier-handed than others. The rice portion is also variable, some hawkers pile on 250g instead of 200g, which adds another 65 calories just like that. Know what you're ordering lah, and you can make this work in a calorie deficit without suffering.

How to eat it lighter

  • Ask for the pot without lap cheong and salted fish, and you drop from 700 to around 530 calories straightaway.
  • Request less rice, maybe 150g instead of 200g, and save yourself about 65 calories without touching the protein.
  • Chicken breast instead of thigh cuts the fat significantly, though not all stalls will accommodate this.
  • Skip eating the chicken skin, which carries a chunk of the fat in that 220-calorie thigh portion.
  • Share a large pot with someone instead of ordering solo, then bulk up with a side of plain stir-fried veg to stay full without adding many calories.

Common questions

How many calories in Claypot Chicken Rice?
A standard single-serve claypot with chicken, lap cheong, and salted fish is around 700 calories. That's for a typical hawker portion of about 380g total.
Is Claypot Chicken Rice good for weight loss?
It can fit into a weight loss plan, but you need to be aware of what's in it. The lap cheong alone adds 120 calories and quite a bit of fat. If you skip the sausage and salted fish, you drop down to around 530 calories, which is much more manageable. The chicken thigh gives you solid protein, so it's actually fairly filling per calorie.
What makes Claypot Chicken Rice so high in calories?
The combo of lap cheong (Chinese sausage), skin-on chicken thigh, and the cooking oils and sauces adds up fast. The rice itself is 260 calories, then you pile fatty sausage and oily dark soy on top. It's not bad overall, but it's definitely not a low-calorie meal.
Berapa kalori dalam Claypot Chicken Rice?
Claypot Chicken Rice ada kira-kira 700 kalori untuk satu hidangan biasa (380g), dengan 38g protein, 70g karbohidrat dan 27g lemak. Angka ini anggaran untuk hidangan gerai biasa, jadi ia berbeza ikut kedai dan saiz hidangan.
