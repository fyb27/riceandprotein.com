How Many Calories in Fish Ball Noodles?

Also known as Fishball Noodles, Mee Pok Fishball.

A hawker staple of bouncy fish balls over mee, kway teow, or bee hoon, served dry with lard and dark soy or in a clear savoury broth.

390kcal / 1 bowl
A standard bowl of fish ball noodles comes in at around 390 calories for a 350g serving, dry version. That's honestly not bad for a full hawker meal that actually fills you up. The soup version drops slightly to about 370 calories since it uses less lard and lighter sauce. You're getting 20g of protein from the fish balls and a solid 57g of carbs from the noodles, with fat sitting at just 9g. As hawker noodles go, this is one of the more reasonable options on the menu.

The real talk

Fish ball noodles is genuinely one of the friendlier noodle dishes at the hawker centre. At 390 calories for dry and 370 for soup, it's not going to wreck your day. The reason the number stays manageable is simple: fish balls are mostly fish paste and water, not deep fried, not coated in batter. Six pieces at around 120g only comes to 110 calories. The bulk of the calories here is the noodles, 130g of cooked mee or kway teow at 185 calories, which is just carbs doing carb things. The lard oil in the dry version adds 70 calories and a lot of the flavour, bro, let's be honest.

What actually shifts the maths is how much lard the uncle uses and whether you go soup or dry. Soup version saves you about 20 calories because the condiments are lighter and there's less oil. Ask for less oil on the dry version and you could probably shave another 30 to 40 calories off. The other variable is add-ons: most stalls let you top up with extra fish balls or a slice of fish cake. Two extra fish balls or one slice of fish cake pushes you to around 450 calories, which is still not crazy, but it adds up if you're tracking. Stick to the standard six fish balls, go soup if you can, and you've got a 370-calorie lunch that actually keeps you full.

How to eat it lighter

  • Order soup instead of dry: saves roughly 20 calories and you still get a full satisfying bowl.
  • Ask the uncle for less oil or no lard on dry version: cutting that 8g of lard drops about 60 to 70 calories.
  • Skip the fish cake or extra fish ball add-ons: each fish cake slice adds around 60 to 80 calories.
  • Choose bee hoon over yellow mee if available: similar calories but lighter texture and usually a slightly smaller portion.
  • Eat it as a standalone meal without a sugary drink on the side: teh tarik adds another 100 plus calories and most people forget to count it.

Is soup or dry fish ball noodles lower in calories?
