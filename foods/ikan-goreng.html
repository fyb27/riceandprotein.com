How Many Calories in Ikan Goreng?

Ikan Goreng is your everyday Malaysian fried fish, rubbed with turmeric and a little flour then deep-fried until the skin goes crisp and golden.

230kcal / 1 piece (medium, e.g. kembung, tilapia slice, or cencaru)
One medium piece of ikan goreng, around 140g, comes in at roughly 230 calories with 25g protein and 12g fat. The fish itself is lean, so the calories mostly come from frying oil absorbed into the skin and that thin flour coating. If you are grabbing kembung from a nasi campur stall, expect around 180 calories for the smaller whole fish. Go for a big siakap fillet fried with thick batter and you are looking at 350 calories or more.

The real talk

Ikan goreng is one of those dishes that sounds healthy because, well, it is fish. And honestly it is not bad. The fish flesh itself is lean protein, whether you are eating kembung (Indian mackerel), tilapia, or cencaru. The problem is the frying. About 80 of those 230 calories come purely from oil absorbed during frying, and that number is a ballpark estimate because solid nutrition data for Malaysian hawker-style fried fish is thin. Different fish sizes, oil temperatures, and how long it sits in the pan all change things. Treat 230 as a reasonable middle-ground guess, not gospel.

What really moves the calorie count is the fish size and what is stuffed into it. A small kembung goreng runs about 180 calories. A standard tilapia slice is around 230. Cencaru sumbat sambal, where they stuff a spiced chili-herb paste inside the whole fish, jumps to around 290 calories because of that filling. A big siakap goreng tepung with thick batter coating hits 350 calories, because more batter means way more oil absorbed. If you are trying to keep things tighter, stick to smaller fish or request it without the thick flour batter, and eat the flesh while leaving the skin (where most of the oil lives) on the plate. Pair it with rice and kangkung rather than lauk that come with heavy gravy, and you have a solid high-protein meal without doing too much damage.

How to eat it lighter

  • Choose kembung or cencaru over big battered fillets like siakap goreng tepung. You save 100-170 calories just by picking the smaller fish.
  • Skip the thick flour batter. A light turmeric dusting absorbs far less oil than a proper coating.
  • Eat the flesh and leave the crispy skin. Most of the absorbed frying oil is concentrated in the skin.
  • Drain it on a paper towel or newspaper before eating, the hawker way actually does reduce surface oil a bit.
  • Pair with steamed dishes like ikan stim or sayur campur instead of another oil-heavy lauk, so the whole plate stays reasonable.

Common questions

How many calories in Ikan Goreng?
A medium piece of ikan goreng, around 140g, is approximately 230 calories. A small kembung goreng is around 180 calories, while a large battered fillet like siakap goreng tepung can hit 350 calories.
What exactly is Ikan Goreng?
Ikan goreng just means fried fish in Malay. At a nasi campur or nasi kandar stall you will usually see whole small fish like kembung or cencaru, or cut slices of tilapia, rubbed with turmeric and sometimes a little flour, then deep-fried until crisp. It is one of the most common side dishes in Malaysian everyday cooking.
Is Ikan Goreng healthy for weight loss?
It is decent, especially compared to other lauk. You get 25g of protein per piece, which is solid. The catch is 12g of fat, mostly from frying oil. If you stick to smaller fish without thick batter, it is one of the better protein options at a nasi campur stall. Just watch what else you pile on the plate.
Berapa kalori dalam Ikan Goreng?
Ikan Goreng ada kira-kira 230 kalori untuk 1 keping (140g), dengan 25g protein, 4g karbohidrat dan 12g lemak. Angka ini anggaran untuk hidangan gerai biasa, jadi ia berbeza ikut kedai dan saiz hidangan.
