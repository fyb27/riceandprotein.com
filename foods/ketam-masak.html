How Many Calories in Ketam Masak?

A classic Malaysian crab dish cooked in sambal, chili, coconut milk, or salted egg sauce, with calories that swing wildly depending on which version lands on your table.

300kcal / 1 serving (about half a crab with sauce)
Ketam Masak comes in at around 300 calories per serving (about half a crab with sauce, roughly 200g total). That number sits in the middle of the pack for Malaysian lauk dishes. The crab meat itself is only about 70 calories for 70g, which is impressively lean. What pushes the total up is the cooking oil from the hawker stir-fry (around 130 calories) and the sambal or chili paste (about 80 calories). So you are basically eating a high-protein, moderately calorie dish where most of the calories come from the sauce, not the crab itself.

The real talk

Ketam Masak sits at around 300 calories for a standard hawker serving of half a crab with sambal sauce, and honestly that is not bad at all for a lauk. The crab meat itself is only about 70 calories for 70g, which makes it one of the leaner proteins you can order. The problem is the 15g of cooking oil the hawker uses for that high-heat wok fry, which alone contributes roughly 130 calories. Add in the sambal paste at about 80 calories and you have a dish where the sauce costs you more than the protein. Fair warning though, these numbers are a ballpark estimate. Hard nutrition data for Malaysian crab dishes is thin, and every hawker cooks differently, so treat 300 calories as a reasonable middle-ground figure, not a lab result.

What actually changes the maths is the variant you order and how much gravy you eat. Masak Cili is the lightest at around 280 calories because it is drier with less oil. Masak Lemak jumps to 380 calories because santan (coconut milk) is basically liquid fat. And Masak Telur Masin? That one hits 450 calories because butter and salted egg yolks all pile in at once. The other big variable is portion size. If you are at a restaurant and they give you a full crab instead of half, double everything lah. Eat the crab meat, go easy on mopping up the sauce with your rice, and you will be fine. At 22g of protein per serving, this is actually one of the better protein-per-calorie options in the lauk lineup.

How to eat it lighter

  • Order masak cili or masak sambal instead of masak lemak or salted egg, you save yourself 80 to 150 calories right there.
  • Do not use bread or extra rice to soak up the sauce. The gravy is where most of the calories hide.
  • Ask the hawker for less oil if you are ordering at a stall that cooks to order. Most will accommodate if you ask nicely.
  • Eat the crab meat and leave most of the thick gravy in the bowl. You still get all the flavour and most of the protein.
  • Order one crab between two people as a shared lauk alongside vegetables, so you keep your portion to roughly half a crab per person.

Common questions

How many calories in Ketam Masak?
A standard serving of Ketam Masak (about half a crab with sauce, roughly 200g) is around 300 calories. That is for the masak sambal version. Masak lemak runs closer to 380 calories and masak telur masin can hit 450 calories, so the variant you order makes a real difference.
Is Ketam Masak a good choice when dieting?
It is actually decent, bro. The crab meat itself is very lean at around 70 calories for 70g and gives you 22g of protein per serving. The calories come mainly from the cooking oil and sauce, not the protein. Stick to masak cili or masak sambal, skip mopping up the gravy with rice, and you are looking at a solid high-protein lauk.
Which version of Ketam Masak has the most calories?
Masak Telur Masin (salted egg butter crab) is the heaviest at around 450 calories per serving. The butter and salted egg yolks stack up fast. Masak Lemak comes second at around 380 calories because of the coconut milk. If you want the lightest option, go for Masak Cili at around 280 calories.
