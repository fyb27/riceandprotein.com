How Many Calories in Ketupat Sotong?

Ketupat Sotong is a Terengganu and Kelantan specialty where whole squid is stuffed with glutinous rice then simmered low and slow in a sweet, rich coconut milk gravy.

590kcal / 1 medium squid stuffed with glutinous rice in coconut gravy
One medium ketupat sotong comes in at around 590 calories, with 80g of carbs, 21g of protein, and 19g of fat. That is for a single stuffed squid piece sitting in its coconut gravy, roughly 310g total. Not a light dish by any stretch, but the squid itself is actually lean protein doing honest work. The calories are being driven by the pulut stuffing and the santan gravy around it.

The real talk

Ketupat sotong is one of those east coast dishes that most Malaysians from KL or the west have never actually tried. Picture a whole squid body, cleaned and packed tight with raw glutinous rice, then sealed and braised in coconut milk sweetened with gula melaka until the pulut cooks inside the squid and absorbs all that gravy. The result is dense, sticky, sweet, and honestly very good. But at 590 calories for one piece, it earns its place on the higher end of the calorie list. The pulut stuffing alone accounts for about 240 calories, the thick santan gravy adds another 178, and the palm sugar in that same gravy throws in 80 more. The squid itself is only 92 calories. So you are really paying for the rice and the coconut sauce, not the seafood. Fair warning though: these numbers are a genuine ballpark estimate. Hard nutrition data for ketupat sotong is almost nonexistent, and portion sizes vary a lot between home cooking and pasar malam stalls, so treat this as a rough guide rather than a precise count.

What actually moves the maths here is gravy portion and squid size. A large squid stuffed more generously could easily push past 700 calories, while a smaller piece from a pasar ramadan stall might land closer to 450 to 500. If you are eating this at a kenduri or during Raya and someone ladles extra gravy on your plate, that 55g of coconut milk sauce in our estimate can quickly become 90 to 100g, adding another 100 calories right there. There is no real way to make a lighter version at a stall since you eat what you are served. If you are cooking at home, using light coconut milk (santan pekat campur air) instead of full-fat santan can bring the gravy calories down by roughly 40 to 50 calories per serving, and going easier on the gula melaka saves another 20 to 30. The squid is not the problem at all, bro. It is the stuff around it.

How to eat it lighter

  • Ask for less gravy when possible, because that thick santan is where the bulk of the fat calories live. Even dropping from 55g to 30g of gravy saves around 80 calories.
  • Eat it without rice on the side. The pulut inside the squid is already your carb load for this meal, adding a plate of nasi on top pushes you well past 900 calories.
  • If cooking at home, swap full-fat santan for a diluted version, one part thick coconut milk to one part water. Same flavour profile, noticeably lighter gravy.
  • Pair it with ulam-ulaman or raw vegetables on the side rather than another heavy lauk. You get volume and fibre without adding significant calories.
  • One piece is genuinely a full meal. This is not a side dish, it is a main. Treat it that way and you will not feel the need to keep reaching for more.

Common questions

How many calories in Ketupat Sotong?
One medium ketupat sotong with coconut gravy is around 590 calories. That covers the squid body at 92 cal, the glutinous rice stuffing at 240 cal, the thick santan gravy at 178 cal, and the palm sugar in the gravy at 80 cal, for a total of roughly 310g of food.
What exactly is Ketupat Sotong?
It is a traditional dish from Terengganu and Kelantan where a whole squid is stuffed with raw glutinous rice, then simmered in sweetened coconut milk until the rice cooks inside the squid body. The result is a sticky, dense, slightly sweet dish usually eaten during Raya or at east coast pasar malam stalls.
Is Ketupat Sotong healthy?
The squid itself is lean and decent in protein at 21g per serving, which is fine. The problem is the glutinous rice and the sweet coconut gravy pushing the carbs to 80g and the overall calories to 590 for one piece. It is not something you want to eat daily if you are watching your intake, but as an occasional Raya dish it is absolutely worth it.
