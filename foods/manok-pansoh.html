How Many Calories in Manok Pansoh?

Manok Pansoh is a Sarawak Iban and Bidayuh chicken dish cooked inside a sealed bamboo tube over an open fire with lemongrass, ginger, garlic, torch ginger flower and cassava leaves, producing a fragrant, brothy result with almost no added oil.

210kcal / 1 serving (mixed bone-in chicken pieces with broth and aromatics)
One serving of Manok Pansoh (about 280g of bone-in chicken pieces, broth and aromatics) comes in at roughly 210 calories, with 32g protein, 7g carbs and 5g fat. That 5g of fat is mostly from the chicken itself, not from any oil added during cooking, because the bamboo seals everything in and the chicken essentially steams in its own juices. For a proper lauk, those are genuinely good numbers.

The real talk

Okay so first, let me put you on to this dish if you haven't had it before. Manok Pansoh is a traditional Iban and Bidayuh dish from Sarawak where chicken gets packed into a fresh bamboo tube with lemongrass, ginger, galangal, shallots, garlic, bungai kantan (torch ginger flower) and cassava leaves (pucuk ubi), then the whole thing goes over a fire until it's cooked. No wok, no oil, no frying. The bamboo keeps everything moist and adds a faint smoky, grassy aroma that you cannot replicate any other way. At 210 calories for a 280g serving, most of that comes from the 140g of bone-in chicken (175 cal). The broth is basically free at 20 cal, the pucuk ubi adds 12 cal and the aromatics are 5 cal. It's genuinely one of the leanest ways to eat chicken you'll find in any Malaysian cuisine. Fair warning though: these numbers are a ballpark estimate because hard nutritional data for traditional Borneo dishes is thin, so treat 210 as an honest best guess rather than a lab-verified figure.

What actually moves the number is the chicken cut. If you're getting a version with a lot of thigh or drumstick versus mostly breast, the fat can creep up a bit, maybe 230 to 250 calories per serving instead of 210. The broth is not really an issue calorie-wise unless someone has added coconut milk to it, which is not traditional but does happen at certain restaurants, and that can push it closer to 280 to 320 calories. Portion size matters too: the standard festival or warung serving is roughly 280g but if you're getting a generous plate with extra broth, you might be looking at 350g, which would put you around 260 calories. The good news is there's basically no way to make this dish high-calorie accidentally if you're eating it in its traditional form, no oil, no coconut milk, bone-in skinless chicken, lots of aromatics and cassava leaves.

How to eat it lighter

  • Ask for breast-heavy pieces if you can. Bone-in breast in pansoh style will keep you closer to 180 to 190 calories per serving versus thigh-heavy cuts.
  • Skip the skin if there is any left on. Some versions include a bit of skin for flavour during cooking; pull it off before eating and you shave off maybe 20 to 30 calories.
  • Drink the broth, skip extra rice if you're watching carbs. The broth is essentially 20 calories and packed with aromatics, so it fills you up without doing damage.
  • At a restaurant, ask if coconut milk was added. Traditional pansoh has none. Coconut milk in the broth is the one thing that turns this from a diet-friendly dish into something you need to account for properly.
  • Pair it with ulam-ulaman or paku pakis on the side instead of extra rice. You get volume, fibre and more micronutrients without adding many calories.

Common questions

How many calories in Manok Pansoh?
A standard serving of Manok Pansoh (about 280g, bone-in chicken with broth and aromatics) has around 210 calories, with 32g protein, 7g carbs and 5g fat. These are estimates because solid nutritional data for this traditional Sarawak dish is hard to find, but 210 is a reasonable ballpark.
What exactly is Manok Pansoh and is it healthy?
Manok Pansoh is a traditional Iban and Bidayuh dish from Sarawak where chicken is cooked inside a bamboo tube over fire with lemongrass, ginger, garlic, torch ginger flower and cassava leaves. Because there's no added oil and the chicken steams in its own juices, it's genuinely one of the healthiest ways to eat chicken in Malaysian cooking. High protein, low fat, no deep frying involved.
How does Manok Pansoh compare calorie-wise to other chicken dishes?
Manok Pansoh at 210 calories per serving is on the lower end for a chicken lauk. Ayam goreng (fried chicken) can be 280 to 350 calories for a similar weight. Kari ayam with coconut milk gravy easily hits 300 to 400 calories per serving. Pansoh's no-oil bamboo cooking method genuinely keeps the calorie count low compared to most other ways Malaysians cook chicken.
Berapa kalori dalam Manok Pansoh?
Manok Pansoh ada kira-kira 210 kalori untuk 1 hidangan (280g), dengan 32g protein, 7g karbohidrat dan 5g lemak. Angka ini anggaran untuk hidangan gerai biasa, jadi ia berbeza ikut kedai dan saiz hidangan.
