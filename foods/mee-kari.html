How Many Calories in Mee Kari (Curry Mee)?

A Malaysian coconut curry noodle soup that tastes heavier than it actually is, available at hawker stalls and kopitiams everywhere in chicken, prawn, or seafood versions.

520kcal / 1 bowl
A standard bowl of Mee Kari (Curry Mee) comes in at around 520 calories for a typical 420g hawker serving. That number sits in the middle of the pack for Malaysian noodle dishes, mainly because of the coconut curry broth pulling about 145 of those calories on its own. The noodles add another 175, your protein source around 120, and the tofu puffs, bean sprouts, and egg round it out. Not a light meal, but not the worst thing you could order either.

The real talk

Mee Kari sits at around 520 calories for a standard bowl, and honestly that's not terrible for a full meal with noodles, protein, and a rich broth. The fat count is 24g though, and almost all of it comes from the santan (coconut milk) in the curry broth. That 200ml of broth alone is clocking 145 calories, which is the same as a whole small bowl of plain rice. Worth knowing. The 20g of protein is decent too, especially if your stall is generous with the chicken or prawns. Fair warning though: these are estimates, bro. Mee kari doesn't have the kind of standardised recipe data that lab-tested packaged food has, so treat 520 calories as a ballpark, not a certified figure. Stall-to-stall variation is real.

What actually changes the numbers is the broth and the oil. Some stalls add a ladle of chili oil on top, that can quietly add another 80 to 100 calories you didn't see coming. A thicker, richer santan base versus a watered-down one can swing 50 to 80 calories easily. Swapping from chicken (roughly 120 calories per 80g portion) to prawns doesn't change much, but if you go seafood mix with fish cake and squid added in, you're looking at the 560-calorie variant. The noodle portion matters too. If your uncle's stall does a generous 150g of yellow mee instead of 120g, that's an extra 40 to 50 calories right there. Ask for less noodles and more bean sprouts if you're counting lah.

How to eat it lighter

  • Ask them to go easy on the extra chili oil that some stalls ladle on top, that alone can save you 80 to 100 calories.
  • Request less noodles and more bean sprouts. You still get a full bowl, but you swap 40 to 50 calories of starchy noodles for near-zero-calorie veg.
  • Choose the Penang white curry mee version if available, it runs about 480 calories because the broth uses less coconut milk and chili paste.
  • Go chicken over seafood mix if you're watching fat intake. The difference is about 40 calories and the chicken version usually has less oil floating on top.
  • Don't drink all the broth. The last 50ml at the bottom of the bowl is where most of the fat pools. Leaving it is an easy win.

Common questions

How many calories in Mee Kari (Curry Mee)?
A standard hawker bowl of Mee Kari is around 520 calories for approximately 420g. The coconut curry broth accounts for about 145 of those calories, the yellow mee noodles around 175, and the protein and garnishes make up the rest. Variants range from 480 calories (Penang white curry) to 560 calories (mixed seafood).
Is Mee Kari fattening?
It depends on your daily budget. At 520 calories it fits fine in a 1500 to 1800 calorie day as a main meal. The issue is the 24g of fat from the coconut milk broth, which is higher than a lot of other noodle soups. It's not the enemy, but if you're eating two bowls or having it with a sugary drink, that's where the numbers start adding up.
Is Mee Kari the same as Curry Laksa?
They're very similar and the names get used interchangeably a lot, but there are small differences. Mee Kari typically uses yellow egg noodles and a slightly less santan-heavy broth. Curry Laksa often has a creamier, richer coconut milk base and uses a wider mix of noodles. Calorie-wise they're in the same range, around 520 to 600 per bowl depending on the stall.
