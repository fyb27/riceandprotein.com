How Many Calories in Paku Pakis?

Pucuk paku fern shoots stir-fried with belacan or sambal, a classic veg dish across Sabah, Sarawak and the East Coast that punches way above its weight on flavour.

180kcal / 1 plate, stir-fried (goreng belacan / sambal)
A standard plate of paku pakis goreng belacan comes in at around 180 calories for 170g. That gets you 9g protein, 14g carbs and 9g fat. Not bad at all for something that tastes that good with rice. The fern shoots themselves are barely 44 calories for 130g cooked, so the oil doing the stir-frying is where most of the calories come from. The protein figure of 9g is on the higher end for a veg dish, but hawker-style goreng belacan typically includes dried shrimp paste and sometimes ikan bilis, which bump up the protein compared to plain blanched ferns.

The real talk

Paku pakis is stir-fried fiddlehead fern shoots, called pucuk paku, and if you have never tried it, think of it like kangkung's wilder Bornean cousin. Slightly more tender, earthy, with that classic belacan smell that will make the whole table hungry. At 180 calories a plate it is genuinely one of the smarter veg dishes you can order at a warung. The fern shoots themselves only clock in at 44 calories for 130g, which is almost embarrassingly low. Worth noting though, these numbers assume a hawker goreng belacan that includes shrimp paste with some dried shrimp content. Plain blanched paku pakis without belacan will have noticeably less protein. Take the figures as a solid guide, not a lab result.

What actually moves the maths is the oil and whether santan gets involved. A hawker stall goreng will use roughly 1.5 tbsp of oil (14g, about 90 calories) and that alone accounts for half the plate total. Go kerabu style, blanched with lime and belacan and no frying, and the whole thing drops to around 90 calories. Order masak lemak and coconut milk pushes it up to 260 calories, and if there are prawns or ikan bilis in there too you are looking at 310 calories. Portion size matters too, warung plates vary, so if they pile it on generously add another 20 to 30 calories to whatever version you order.

How to eat it lighter

  • Order it kerabu style if the stall offers it, blanched and tossed with lime and belacan, cuts the calories roughly in half to around 90 calories.
  • Skip the masak lemak version when you are watching your numbers, coconut milk adds 80 to 130 extra calories compared to the goreng belacan version.
  • Eat it with plain white rice rather than nasi goreng so you are not doubling up on oil at the same meal.
  • Ask the stall to go easy on the oil if you are ordering at a sit-down place, some will actually do it if you ask nicely.
  • Use it to bulk out your plate volume without adding many calories, paku pakis is genuinely filling for how low-cal it is.

Common questions

How many calories in Paku Pakis?
A standard plate of paku pakis goreng belacan is around 180 calories for 170g. The kerabu salad version drops to about 90 calories, while masak lemak with santan climbs to 260 to 310 calories depending on what protein is added.
What exactly is paku pakis?
Paku pakis refers to fiddlehead fern shoots, called pucuk paku, that are stir-fried with belacan (shrimp paste), sambal or cooked in coconut milk gravy. It is a staple veg dish in Sabah, Sarawak and parts of the East Coast peninsula, and pretty much everyone who tries it becomes a fan.
Is paku pakis a healthy choice when eating out?
Honestly, yes, it is one of the better veg orders you can make. The fern shoots themselves are very low calorie, and the protein adds up when hawker-style belacan with dried shrimp content is in the mix. The main thing to watch is whether it is fried in a lot of oil or cooked in santan, both of which push the calories up considerably. Goreng belacan style at 180 calories is a solid call.
Berapa kalori dalam Paku Pakis?
Paku Pakis ada kira-kira 180 kalori untuk satu hidangan biasa (170g), dengan 9g protein, 14g karbohidrat dan 9g lemak. Angka ini anggaran untuk hidangan gerai biasa, jadi ia berbeza ikut kedai dan saiz hidangan.
