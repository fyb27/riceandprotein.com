How Many Calories in Pinasakan?

A traditional Kadazandusun fish dish from Sabah, small oily fish simmered with turmeric and a souring agent until tangy and broth-y, with barely any oil in sight.

240kcal / 1 serving (2 small fish + broth)
Pinasakan comes in at around 240 calories per serving (2 small fish plus broth, about 230g total). That's for the classic basung or mackerel scad version, simmered in turmeric and a souring agent like takob-akob or tamarind, no frying involved. Almost all of those 240 calories are coming from the fish itself, which is why this dish punches way above its weight for protein, 34g of it.

The real talk

Pinasakan is a Kadazandusun staple from Sabah that most peninsular Malaysians have never heard of, and honestly that's their loss. The dish is simple: small fish (usually basung, which is mackerel scad) simmered with turmeric, lemongrass, ginger, and a souring agent, the traditional choice being takob-akob, a wild fruit that grows in Sabah, though bambangan or tamarind works too. No coconut milk, no frying, no heavy rempah. The result is a tangy, lightly brothy fish dish that tastes way more interesting than it sounds. At 240 calories for two fish plus broth, the calorie count is genuinely low, and 34g of protein from one lauk is better than most things on a nasi campur spread. Fair warning though: these numbers are a ballpark estimate because hard nutritional data on Pinasakan is basically nonexistent. The figures here are built from the fish species and cooking method, treat them as a reasonable guide rather than a lab result.

What actually moves the numbers is the fish size and whether anyone decided to fry the fish first before simmering, which some cooks do. If you get a larger serving or bigger fish (say tenggiri instead of basung), you're looking at closer to 280 calories. The broth itself contributes almost nothing calorie-wise, maybe 10 to 15 calories, so drinking the broth is fine. Where people wreck the maths is on the rice side: most Sabahans eat this with a generous plate of rice, so add 200 to 350 calories depending on your portion lah. Pinasakan as a lauk is one of the easiest high-protein, low-fat choices you can make at a Sabah spread. The rice is where you have to use your head.

How to eat it lighter

  • Eat it with less rice. The fish itself is already low cal, so don't undermine it with three scoops of nasi.
  • Ask for less broth if you're eating out and the cook adds salt generously.
  • Stick to the sada version with basung rather than upgrading to tenggiri, which adds another 40 calories per serving.
  • Pair it with sayur manis or tuhau on the side instead of a fried lauk so your whole plate stays reasonable.
  • If cooking at home, use fresh takob-akob or tamarind rather than packaged paste, which sometimes has added sugar.

Common questions

How many calories in Pinasakan?
A standard serving of Pinasakan (2 small basung fish plus broth, around 230g) comes to about 240 calories, with 34g protein and only 9g fat. It's one of the lighter fish dishes you'll find in Sabah cooking.
What is Pinasakan exactly?
Pinasakan is a traditional Kadazandusun dish from Sabah. Small fish, usually basung (mackerel scad), are simmered with turmeric, lemongrass, ginger, and a sour ingredient like takob-akob (a wild fruit) or bambangan (a Sabah wild mango). No deep frying, no thick coconut gravy. It's broth-y, tangy, and very simple. If you've only eaten peninsular fish dishes, think of it as the Sabah answer to asam pedas but much lighter.
Is Pinasakan good for weight loss?
Honestly, yes. For 240 calories you get 34g of protein, which is solid. The cooking method uses no added oil to speak of, and the souring agent keeps the broth light instead of rich. The main thing to watch is your rice portion on the side, because nobody's eating Pinasakan without rice.
