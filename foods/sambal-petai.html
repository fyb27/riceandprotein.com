How Many Calories in Sambal Petai?

Petai (stink beans) stir-fried in sambal tumis with prawns or ikan bilis, one of those dishes that smells intense but tastes absolutely worth it.

400kcal / 1 hawker plate (sambal petai with prawns)
A hawker plate of sambal petai with prawns is around 400 calories for roughly 190g. Protein sits at 22g, carbs at 18g, and fat at 26g. The stink beans themselves are not the problem at about 85 calories for 60g. The real driver is the sambal oil, roughly 25ml of cooking oil in there adding about 205 calories on its own. The prawns chip in around 80 calories and a solid chunk of that protein.

The real talk

Sambal petai is one of those dishes people either love immediately or need one convincing bite to get hooked on. The beans smell funky, yes, but the flavour is earthy and strong in a way that makes the whole sambal hit differently. Calorie-wise, 400 calories for a plate is not outrageous for a lauk, especially when you are getting 22g of protein from the prawns. The problem is this dish almost always comes with rice, so you are easily at 650 to 700 calories by the time you sit down. Worth knowing before you go for a second scoop. Fair warning though: these numbers are a ballpark estimate because solid nutritional data for sambal petai is genuinely thin. Cooking methods and oil amounts vary a lot from one stall to the next.

What actually swings the calories is how heavy-handed the cook is with the oil. A stingy tablespoon versus a generous glug can be the difference between 300 and 450 calories on the same plate. The version with ikan bilis instead of prawns comes in around 360 calories, while swapping to beef pushes it to about 430 calories. If you want to keep it lighter, the petai and prawns are not your enemy at roughly 165 calories combined. It is the 205 calories sitting in that 25ml of sambal oil that you need to think about. At a good stall you cannot control the oil, bah, but at home you can cut it to 15ml and save around 80 calories without killing the dish.

How to eat it lighter

  • Ask for less gravy or sambal on the side if ordering at a stall that does custom plating, the oil is the main calorie driver.
  • At home, cut the cooking oil from 25ml down to 12 to 15ml and use a non-stick wok, you save 80 to 100 calories just like that.
  • Go with the ikan bilis version instead of prawns and you drop about 40 calories per plate, plus ikan bilis is cheaper lah.
  • Pair it with less rice or switch to a smaller scoop, the sambal is strong enough to carry even a modest portion of rice.
  • Eat it as a shared lauk with other dishes rather than as the main solo protein, you naturally end up eating less of the oily sambal that way.

Common questions

How many calories in Sambal Petai?
A standard hawker plate of sambal petai with prawns is around 400 calories for about 190g. The ikan bilis version is slightly lower at around 360 calories, and the beef version comes in at around 430 calories.
What exactly is sambal petai?
Petai are stink beans, flat green beans that grow in long pods and have a strong, pungent smell. Sambal petai is those beans stir-fried in sambal tumis, a cooked chili paste made with dried chilies, shallots, belacan, and tamarind. Usually comes with prawns or ikan bilis.
Is sambal petai actually healthy or is it just oily lauk?
Honest answer: it is somewhere in between. The petai beans themselves have decent fibre and the prawns give you solid lean protein. The issue is the sambal oil, which accounts for more than half the calories on the plate. Good dish, just do not pretend the oil is not there.
