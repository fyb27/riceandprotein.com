How Many Calories in Sambal Sotong?

Squid cooked in sambal tumis, a classic Malaysian lauk that's surprisingly decent protein for the calories if you watch the sauce.

270kcal / 1 side dish (lauk) serving
Sambal sotong comes in at around 270 calories for a standard lauk serving of about 200g. That's roughly 130g of cooked squid with a decent scoop of sambal tumis on top. The squid itself is actually pretty lean at around 130 calories, but the sambal sauce (chilli, oil, onion, belacan) adds another 110 calories on top of that. So yeah, it's not the heaviest dish on the table, but the sauce does most of the calorie work here.

The real talk

Sambal sotong is honestly one of the better lauk options if you're watching calories, bro. A standard hawker serving sits at around 270 calories for 200g, and 28g of protein is solid for a side dish. The squid itself is lean, maybe 130 calories for 130g cooked. The rest comes from the sambal tumis, which is where the oil, chilli paste, and belacan gang up on you for about 110 calories. Add petai and you're looking at 310 calories instead. None of these are scary numbers on their own, but fair warning: these are ballpark estimates. Hard calorie data for Malaysian home-cooked and hawker dishes is thin, so treat this as a reasonable guide rather than a lab result.

What actually changes the maths is the sambal. A generous auntie at the nasi campur stall who drowns your sotong in sauce can easily push your 270-calorie serving toward 350 or higher. Ask for less gravy or scoop the squid pieces and leave some sauce behind. On the flip end, sotong bakar (grilled squid with sambal on the side) drops to around 220 calories because you skip the oil absorption from wet cooking. Sotong goreng kunyit goes the other way, up to about 330 calories because deep-frying pulls in more oil than a wet sambal does. The squid itself is doing you a favour with the protein. The cooking method and sauce quantity are what decide whether this is a smart pick or not.

How to eat it lighter

  • Go for sotong bakar if available, grilled squid with sambal on the side comes in at around 220 calories instead of 270.
  • At nasi campur, ask for less sambal gravy and more of the squid pieces. The sauce is where most of the fat lives.
  • Skip the petai if you're being strict. It adds roughly 30 to 40 calories and you probably won't miss it on a deficit day.
  • Pair it with plain rice and a veg side instead of nasi lemak or nasi goreng. The sotong is already flavourful enough that you don't need a fancy base.
  • Watch your total lauk count. Sambal sotong at 270 calories is fine as your one main lauk, but if you're stacking it with rendang and sambal udang on the same plate, the calories add up fast lah.

Common questions

How many calories in Sambal Sotong?
A standard lauk serving of sambal sotong (about 200g) has around 270 calories, with 28g protein, 10g carbs, and 12g fat. The squid contributes roughly 130 calories and the sambal tumis sauce adds another 110 or so.
Is sambal sotong good for weight loss?
It can be, actually. The squid is a lean protein and 270 calories for a lauk isn't bad at all. The thing to watch is the sambal sauce, which carries most of the fat. Ask for less gravy, or go for sotong bakar at around 220 calories if you want the lower-calorie version.
How does sambal sotong compare to sambal udang in calories?
They're in a similar range. Prawns and squid are both lean seafood, so the calorie difference mostly comes down to the sambal quantity and whether extra ingredients like petai are added. Sambal sotong at 270 calories is a reasonable benchmark for either dish.
