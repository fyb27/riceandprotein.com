How Many Calories in Sinalau Bakas?

Sabah's famous smoked wild boar, slow-cooked over fire until the fat renders and the skin gets that deep, smoky bark that you will be thinking about for days.

560kcal / 1 portion sliced smoked wild boar (hawker stall, ~200g)
Sinalau bakas comes in at around 560 calories for a standard 200g hawker portion of fatty shoulder or rib cuts. That is all from protein and fat, zero carbs, so if you are tracking macros it is 44g protein and 40g fat in one sitting. The number jumps to 820 calories once you add rice, which is how most people actually eat it at a kedai kopi. For leaner cuts like leg meat, you are looking at closer to 320 calories for the same 200g weight. Bear in mind this is a ballpark figure since hard nutritional data on smoked wild boar is basically nonexistent, so treat it as a solid estimate rather than a lab result.

The real talk

Sinalau bakas is a Kadazan-Dusun dish from Sabah where wild boar is rubbed with garlic, lemongrass, turmeric and salt, then slow-smoked over a wood fire. If you are from the peninsula, think of it as the Sabah version of babi panggang but wilder, smokier, and more rustic. A typical hawker portion of 200g of fatty shoulder or rib cuts sits at around 560 calories, and bro, it earns every single one of them. The lean muscle itself is only about 210 calories for 130g, but the 50g of rendered fat on the bone brings in another 315 calories on its own. That fat is also exactly why this thing tastes so good, so there is no mystery here. It is non-halal, by the way, so plan accordingly. Hard numbers on smoked wild boar are genuinely scarce, so these figures are a careful estimate based on USDA wild boar data and the actual fat content of typical cuts. Treat it as a solid ballpark, not a lab-certified figure.

What actually moves the number is which cut you get. Lean leg cuts at 200g drop all the way to around 320 calories, basically a different meal lah. Fatty shoulder or rib with the fat cap intact is the standard hawker serve and that is where you hit 560. Add a plate of rice (200g cooked, roughly 260 calories) and the whole meal is about 820 calories, which is fine if that is your one big meal of the day. The sinalau-in-a-bun version at Kota Kinabalu restaurants is actually more calorie-controlled at 480 calories total because you only get about 100g of meat in the bun. If you are watching your intake, ask for the lean cuts specifically or split a portion with someone. There is no gravy, no sauce, no oil added after smoking, so what you see is what you get.

How to eat it lighter

  • Ask specifically for leg or loin cuts instead of shoulder or rib. Same 200g portion drops from 560 to around 320 calories.
  • Split the portion. A 100g serving of the standard fatty cuts is about 280 calories and honestly still very satisfying because the flavour is so intense.
  • Skip the rice or go half portion (100g cooked rice instead of 200g) to save about 130 calories if you are having it as a rice set.
  • Eat it as the protein anchor of your meal and fill the rest of your plate with sayur manis or tuhau. Both are near-zero calories and pair well with the smoky flavour.
  • If you are at a restaurant doing the sinalau bun, that is your best calorie value at 480 calories for a full meal.

Common questions

How many calories in Sinalau Bakas?
A standard 200g hawker portion of sinalau bakas (fatty shoulder or rib cuts) has around 560 calories, with 44g protein and 40g fat and zero carbs. Leaner leg cuts at the same weight come in closer to 320 calories. Add 200g of white rice for the full set and you are at about 820 calories.
What exactly is sinalau bakas?
Sinalau bakas is a traditional Kadazan-Dusun dish from Sabah, East Malaysia. Wild boar (bakas) is rubbed with garlic, lemongrass, turmeric and salt, then slow-smoked over a wood fire. The result is deeply smoky, rich meat with a bark on the outside and tender, fatty flesh inside. It is non-halal.
Is sinalau bakas high in fat?
Yes, the typical hawker portion with shoulder or rib cuts is about 40g of fat per 200g serving, which is high. The fat is all from the animal itself, mostly saturated fat from the rendered pork fat layer. If you want a lower-fat version, ask for leg cuts specifically and the fat content drops significantly, bringing calories down to around 320 for the same portion size.
