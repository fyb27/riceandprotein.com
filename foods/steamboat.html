How Many Calories in Steamboat?

A communal hotpot where the calories swing wildly depending on your broth, protein choices, and whether you ordered noodles or not.

750kcal / 1 serving per person (mixed meats, seafood, noodles, veg, clear or mild broth)
A typical Malaysian steamboat sitting comes in at around 750 calories per person, based on a 650g mix of sliced meats, seafood, noodles, veg, and clear broth with dipping sauce. That figure covers the standard hawker version: a bit of everything in a clear chicken or pork bone broth. Go mala and you are looking at 1,200 calories easy, because the soup base alone is basically chilli oil and lard. Stick to clear broth with lean protein and skip the noodles and you can get it down to 400 calories for the same satisfying meal.

The real talk

Steamboat is one of those meals that feels healthy because you are cooking everything in hot water, but the number can surprise you. The baseline estimate is around 750 calories per person for a standard mixed plate in clear broth, and that is genuinely a decent meal: 48g of protein, 65g of carbs, reasonable fat. The catch is that this is a rough ballpark. Hard data on steamboat is thin because every table orders differently, every shop uses a different recipe, and nobody is weighing their prawns at a steamboat restaurant. So treat 750 as a reasonable middle ground, not a lab result.

What actually moves the number is the broth and the processed stuff. Mala broth can add 400 to 600 calories on its own before you put a single prawn in the pot. The processed items like fish balls, tofu puffs, and crabsticks are sneaky too, about 110 calories in just 60g because they are made with starch and oil. Noodles add another 210 calories for 60g dry weight. If you want to keep steamboat under 500 calories, the formula is simple: clear broth, lean chicken or seafood only, load up the veg (150g of cabbage and mushrooms is only 60 calories), and skip the noodles. You will still leave the table full, bro.

How to eat it lighter

  • Pick clear chicken or pork bone broth, not mala, tom yam, or creamy broth. The broth base alone is the biggest calorie lever at this table.
  • Skip the noodles or take half a portion. Going from 60g dry noodles to nothing saves you 210 calories without you even noticing once the table is full of everything else.
  • Limit processed items like fish balls, tofu puffs, and crabsticks. They are calorie-dense for their size. Load your plate with raw veg instead.
  • Go heavy on prawns, sliced chicken, and fish over pork belly and fatty beef slices. Lean protein fills you up and costs less in fat calories.
  • Watch the dipping sauce. A small dish of sesame paste or peanut sauce can add 80 to 150 calories without you realising it. Use light soy sauce with chilli instead.

Common questions

How many calories in Steamboat?
A standard Malaysian steamboat serving comes in at around 750 calories per person, based on a 650g mix of meats, seafood, noodles, veg, and clear broth. A light version with no noodles and clear broth drops to about 400 calories, while a mala broth version can hit 1,200 calories.
Is steamboat good for weight loss?
It can be, if you control the variables. Clear broth, lean protein, lots of veg, and no noodles gets you a filling 400-calorie meal with decent protein. The problem is most people also order pork belly, processed fish balls, noodles, and a creamy or mala broth. That combination pushes you past 1,000 calories pretty fast.
Why is mala steamboat so high in calories?
The mala soup base is essentially chilli oil, lard, and spices. Before a single piece of meat hits the pot, that base can add 400 to 600 calories per person. If you share the pot with one other person the impact is lower, but solo mala diners are basically drinking flavoured oil throughout the meal.
Berapa kalori dalam Steamboat?
Steamboat ada kira-kira 750 kalori untuk satu hidangan biasa (650g), dengan 48g protein, 65g karbohidrat dan 22g lemak. Angka ini anggaran untuk hidangan gerai biasa, jadi ia berbeza ikut kedai dan saiz hidangan.
