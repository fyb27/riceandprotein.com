How Many Calories in Sukiyaki?

Also known as すき焼き, Japanese Beef Hotpot, Sukiyaki Beef, Sukiyaki Nabe.

Thin sliced beef cooked at the table in a shallow iron pot with tofu, shirataki, napa cabbage, leek and shungiku, in warishita, a sweet sauce of soy, sugar, mirin and sake. Each bite is dipped in raw beaten egg before it goes in your mouth.

455kcal / 1 serving (no rice)
One home style serving of sukiyaki, about 460g with 100g of beef, is around 455 calories on Japanese composition data, before rice. The beef is 220 of that, the warishita sauce with its sugar and mirin is 80, and the raw egg for dipping is 75. Add a 200g bowl of rice and dinner is about 770. A restaurant course with 200g of marbled wagyu is 1,000 or more.

The real talk

Sukiyaki is a beef dish where the sauce is a dessert. Warishita is soy sauce, sugar, mirin and sake in roughly equal parts, and a normal pot takes 50g of it per person, about 80 calories, most of it sugar. Then the beef goes in. Home sukiyaki uses 100g of sliced shoulder or belly at around 220 calories. The vegetables, tofu and shirataki underneath are about 90 for a big pile, which is the part that makes the pot look like more food than it is. The raw egg dip is 75 and it is the best part, so keep it.

The restaurant version is a different budget. A course at a proper sukiyaki house is 150 to 200g of marbled wagyu per person, and A5 ribeye is about 500 calories per 100g against 220 for the supermarket slices. Two hundred grams of that is 1,000 calories of beef alone, plus sauce, plus the rice or udon that goes into the leftover broth at the end. The shime udon, noodles cooked in the sweet remaining sauce, is another 300. A full sukiyaki dinner out is 1,400 to 1,600 and it is worth every one of them, twice a year.

At home it is a good meal. Twenty seven grams of protein, a pile of cabbage and tofu, and 455 calories before rice. The trick is to load the pot with the cheap things. Double the napa, the shirataki and the tofu, keep the beef at 100 to 120g per person, and go light on the second pour of warishita. The egg dip does not change. Sukiyaki made that way with a small bowl of rice is a 650 calorie dinner that tastes like the 1,500 one.

How to eat it lighter

  • Keep the beef at 100 to 120g per person and fill the pot with napa cabbage, shirataki and tofu. The vegetables are 90 calories for a mountain.
  • Use lean sliced shoulder (kata rosu) instead of belly or wagyu. About 220 per 100g versus 400 to 500.
  • Dilute the second pour of warishita with dashi. Each 50g of the sweet sauce is 80 calories, most of it sugar.
  • Skip the shime udon at the end. Noodles cooked in the leftover sauce are 300 calories of sugar soaked starch.
  • One small bowl of rice, 150g, not the 250g restaurant bowl. Saves about 150.

Common questions

How many calories in sukiyaki?
About 455 for a home serving with 100g of beef, no rice. With a bowl of rice it is 770. Restaurant wagyu courses run 1,200 to 1,600.
Is sukiyaki healthy?
Home sukiyaki with lean beef and lots of vegetables is a solid 455 calorie dinner with 27g of protein. The sugar in the sauce and the wagyu fat at restaurants are what push it up.
Sukiyaki or shabu shabu, which is lower?
Shabu shabu by about 150 calories per serving. The beef is swished in plain broth and dipped in ponzu, with no sugar sauce and no raw egg.
How much sugar is in sukiyaki sauce?
Warishita is about 25% sugar plus mirin. A 50g serving carries roughly 15g of sugar, 80 calories.
