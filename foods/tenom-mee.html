How Many Calories in Tenom Mee?

Springy fresh egg noodles from Sabah's Tenom town, tossed in pork lard sauce and served with char siew, eaten dry or in a clear pork broth.

670kcal / 1 bowl (dry tossed)
A bowl of Tenom Mee dry tossed comes in at around 670 calories for a 330g serving. That's a solid number, and it's mostly coming from two places: the fresh egg noodles (280 cal) and the char siew with that pork lard toss sauce (another 375 cal combined). Not a light meal, but not outrageous either for something this filling. Fair warning though, these are ballpark estimates since hard nutrition data on Tenom Mee specifically is basically nonexistent.

The real talk

Tenom Mee is a Sabah thing, specifically from the town of Tenom in the interior. If you've never been to Sabah, think of it as that region's answer to wantan mee: springy fresh egg noodles (sang mee) served dry tossed in a pork lard and soy-based sauce, topped with char siew and a handful of blanched veg. It's non-halal. The dry version sits at around 670 calories for a standard bowl, and honestly that's reasonable for what you're getting, but be clear about what's pushing the number up. The pork lard toss and sauce alone is about 175 calories in a small 25g portion. Add in 80g of char siew at 200 calories and you've already spent 375 cal on the fat and protein before the noodles even show up. These are estimates, bah, because proper nutrition lab data for Tenom Mee basically doesn't exist anywhere. Take the numbers as a solid ballpark, not gospel.

The soup version drops to around 480 calories because you swap the lard toss for a lighter clear pork broth, so that's your easiest win if you're watching calories. The noodle portion itself is the other big lever: 170g of fresh egg noodles is 280 calories, and hawker bowls can easily go heavier than that. If you pile on roasted pork belly and Hakka egg rolls on top of your char siew, you're looking at closer to 820 calories for the mixed toppings version. The veg (choy sum, blanched) is basically free at 15 calories for 60g, so don't skip it. Order extra vegetables if you can, it adds volume without touching the total much.

How to eat it lighter

  • Order the soup version instead of dry tossed: you save roughly 190 calories just by switching from lard toss to clear broth.
  • Ask for less char siew or replace some of it with extra blanched vegetables, the veg runs about 15 calories per 60g versus 200 for the pork.
  • Skip the mixed toppings upgrade (char siew plus siu yuk plus chun kien) and stick to char siew only, saves you around 150 calories.
  • Share a bowl if the hawker gives you a big portion, Tenom Mee served at kopitiam sometimes runs larger than the standard 330g estimate.
  • Don't add extra sauce or ask for less lard drizzle when ordering dry; that pork lard component is where a big chunk of the fat calories hide.

Common questions

How many calories in Tenom Mee?
A standard dry tossed bowl of Tenom Mee is around 670 calories for approximately 330g. The soup version is lighter at about 480 calories. These are estimates since detailed nutritional data for this dish is limited.
What is Tenom Mee?
Tenom Mee is a fresh egg noodle dish from Tenom, a town in Sabah (Malaysian Borneo). The noodles are springy and chewy, served dry tossed in a pork lard and soy-based sauce with char siew (BBQ pork) and blanched vegetables, or alternatively in a clear pork broth. It's a non-halal dish.
Is Tenom Mee healthier than Char Kuey Teow?
The soup version of Tenom Mee at around 480 calories is noticeably lighter than a typical plate of Char Kuey Teow which can hit 700 calories or more. The dry tossed version at 670 calories is roughly comparable. The main difference is the lard toss in the dry version versus the wok hei and lard used in Char Kuey Teow.
