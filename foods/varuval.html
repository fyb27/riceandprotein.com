How Many Calories in Varuval?

Varuval is a South Indian dry-fried dish where chicken or mutton gets coated in a thick spiced masala paste and cooked until dark, fragrant, and clinging.

460kcal / 1 plate (typical mamak/kopitiam serving)
A standard mamak or kopitiam plate of chicken varuval is around 460 calories for roughly 260g. That breaks down to 46g protein, 27g fat, and only 7g carbs. The protein count is genuinely solid, but the fat is real because mamak stalls are not shy with the oil when they fry that masala down.

The real talk

Varuval is a South Indian dry-fried dish you'll find at mamak stalls next to the curry pots. Unlike a curry, there's barely any gravy. Instead, the meat (usually bone-in chicken or mutton) gets cooked in a thick paste of onion, tomato, curry powder, and curry leaves until the masala is almost dry and clinging. It looks modest but these 460 calories are doing real work. About 135 of those calories come purely from absorbed cooking oil, and that's being conservative. To be straight with you, this number is a ballpark estimate because there's almost no hard lab data on varuval specifically. Your actual plate could land anywhere from 400 to 550 depending on how generous the cook was with the oil.

What really moves the numbers is the oil and the portion. A mamak cook frying varuval to order will use a good glug of oil to get that dry, caramelised finish. That 15g of absorbed oil accounts for 135 calories on its own. A home-cooked version using about 2 tbsp of oil for four servings drops you to around 350 calories. Switch to fish varuval (tenggiri or parang) and you're looking at roughly 320 calories because fish absorbs less oil than chicken. Go up to mutton varuval and you're at 530 calories because the meat itself carries more fat before you even count the pan oil. If you're eating this with nasi kandar rice, remember that the rice side is adding another 200 to 300 calories on top.

How to eat it lighter

  • Ask for fish varuval instead of chicken or mutton. Tenggiri is firm, holds up well to the dry fry, and saves you around 140 calories versus the mutton version.
  • If you're cooking at home, use 2 tbsp oil total for four servings and a non-stick pan. You'll still get the dark, dry finish but the absorbed oil drops dramatically.
  • Skip the extra gravy ladled on top. Some stalls add a spoon of curry gravy over varuval before serving. That's another 50 to 80 calories you don't need.
  • Pair it with plain white rice rather than nasi kandar rice, which comes pre-soaked in curry gravy. Saves you 80 to 100 calories right there.
  • Take the bone-in pieces over any boneless request. Bone-in naturally limits how much edible meat you get per piece, which keeps your portion in check without feeling like you're dieting.

Common questions

How many calories in Varuval?
A standard mamak or kopitiam plate of chicken varuval is around 460 calories for a 260g serving. That's roughly 46g protein, 27g fat, and 7g carbs. Mutton varuval runs higher at about 530 calories, while fish varuval is lighter at around 320 calories.
What exactly is varuval?
Varuval is a South Indian dry-fried dish common at Malaysian mamak stalls. The meat (usually chicken or mutton) is cooked with onion, tomato, curry powder, and curry leaves until the masala is thick and almost completely dry. It's spiced, a bit dark in colour, and has no real gravy unlike a standard curry.
Is varuval a good choice if I'm trying to lose weight?
It's decent actually. The protein count at 46g per plate is genuinely high, and the carbs are low at just 7g. The fat is on the higher side at 27g, mostly from cooking oil, but if you're eating varuval instead of a heavy gravy dish poured over rice, you're probably coming out ahead. Fish varuval at around 320 calories is the leaner pick.
Berapa kalori dalam Varuval?
Varuval ada kira-kira 460 kalori untuk 1 pinggan (260g), dengan 46g protein, 7g karbohidrat dan 27g lemak. Angka ini anggaran untuk hidangan gerai biasa, jadi ia berbeza ikut kedai dan saiz hidangan.
