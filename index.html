04 The secret to making it easy

Hunger is the main reason diets fail. When you eat less, your body produces hunger signals. Most people can ignore them for a few days. But not for months. Volume eating solves this problem.

Some foods have a lot of calories packed into a small amount. A tablespoon of oil is 120 calories. A small handful of nuts is 200 calories. Other foods have very few calories but take up a lot of space in the stomach. A large plate of vegetables might be 80 calories. One kilogram of watermelon is around 300 calories.

Volume eating means building your plate out of low calorie foods. 250g of rice. 300g of chicken breast. Then fill the rest with vegetables. That is close to 1 kilogram of food in one sitting. Most people struggle to finish it. But it fits inside a normal calorie target.

The stomach does not count calories. It measures volume. Fill it with the right foods, and hunger stays low even inside a deficit. That is the whole method.

05 What to eat

Every plate needs three things.

Carbs give the body energy. Rice and potato are good choices. They are filling and have a medium calorie density. Not so high that they blow your target, not so low that they leave you hungry.

Protein protects muscle. When the body is in a deficit, it looks for fuel anywhere it can find it. If protein is too low, it breaks down muscle for energy. Hitting your protein target every day prevents this.

Vegetables add volume with almost no calories. A large pile of broccoli has fewer calories than a small handful of cashews. Eat as much as you want.

A simple formula that works: 200-250g rice, 250-300g chicken breast, and as many vegetables as you can fit on the plate. That is close to 1kg of food per sitting, and it fits inside most calorie targets.

06 Why you should lift

When the body is in a calorie deficit, it burns both fat and muscle for fuel. Without resistance training, a significant portion of the weight lost comes from muscle, not fat. The number on the scale drops, but the shape of the body stays roughly the same.

Resistance training sends a signal to the body to keep the muscle. The deficit burns the fat. The training protects the muscle. The result at the same weight is a completely different body shape.

There is a second benefit. Muscle raises your TDEE. A body with more muscle burns more calories at rest. That means you can eat more food and still lose fat at the same rate.

Women do not need to worry about becoming bulky. Building large amounts of muscle requires years of training and specific conditions. Three sessions per week will improve strength and shape, not create bulk.

If the gym feels too far away right now, start with the diet. Nutrition does most of the work. Add lifting when you are ready.
