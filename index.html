How to get started

Hunger is the main reason diets fail. When you eat less, your body produces hunger signals. Most people can ignore them for a few days. But not for months. Volume eating solves this problem.

Some foods have a lot of calories packed into a small amount. A tablespoon of oil is 120 calories. A small handful of nuts is 200 calories. Other foods have very few calories but take up a lot of space in the stomach. A large plate of vegetables might be 80 calories. One kilogram of watermelon is around 300 calories.

Volume eating means building your plate out of low calorie foods. 250g of rice. 300g of chicken breast. Then fill the rest with vegetables. That is close to 1 kilogram of food in one sitting. Most people struggle to finish it. But it fits inside a normal calorie target.

The stomach does not count calories. It measures volume. Fill it with the right foods, and hunger stays low even inside a deficit. That is the whole method.

Should I do cardio or lift weights?

Short answer: lift weights. If you only have time for one, pick lifting every single time.

Here is why. In a calorie deficit you lose weight either way. But your body burns muscle along with the fat unless you give it a reason not to. Lifting is that reason. Cardio is not. So two people can lose the exact same weight and end up looking completely different. One soft, one lean.

Cardio still has its place. It is good for your heart and it burns a few extra calories if you want a bigger deficit. But it is not what changes your shape. Do it as a bonus, not as the main event.

What to do at the gym

When you are in a deficit, your body burns both fat and muscle. Lifting tells it to keep the muscle. The deficit handles the fat. Same weight on the scale, completely different shape.

Three days a week is enough to start. Monday, Wednesday, Friday. Full body each session. You are in and out in under an hour.

Pick one exercise per muscle group, 3 sets of 8 to 12 reps. These five cover everything: Squat (legs), Romanian deadlift (hamstrings and lower back), Bench press (chest), Bent-over row (back), Shoulder press (shoulders).

The one rule that matters: add a little more weight every week. Even 2.5kg. This is called progressive overload. Your body only changes when you give it a reason to. When you can hit 12 reps comfortably, add weight and work back up.

If you can train 5 to 6 days, switch to Push Pull Legs. Push covers chest, shoulders and triceps. Pull covers back and biceps. Legs covers quads, hamstrings and calves. You hit each muscle twice a week which builds faster.

Women, three sessions a week changes your shape, it does not make you bulky. That takes years of very specific effort to achieve.
