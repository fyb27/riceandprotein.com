How Long Does It Take to Lose 10, 20 or 50 Pounds?

Diet Talk Fat Loss

Setting a target with a real date attached is one of the best things you can do for motivation. So here are honest timelines for 10, 20 and 50 pounds, plus the calorie maths so you can work out your own.

Picking a number to lose is easy. Knowing how long it should take is where people either set themselves up to win or set themselves up to quit in week three when they have not hit some impossible target. So let me give you real numbers.

First, the maths everything is built on: one pound of fat is about 3,500 calories, and one kilogram is about 7,700 calories. To lose weight you need a calorie deficit, meaning you eat less than you burn. Add up enough daily deficit and it becomes pounds on the scale.

The sustainable rate

For most people, 1 to 2 pounds per week (about 0.5 to 1 kg) is the rate that is mostly fat and mostly keepable. That comes from a daily deficit of roughly 500 to 1,000 calories. Go faster than that and you start losing muscle, your hunger becomes unmanageable, and you bail. If you have a lot to lose you will lose faster at the start, because heavier bodies burn more and shed more water early. The leaner you get, the slower it goes. That is normal, not a plateau.

The timelines

GoalAt 1 lb/weekAt 2 lb/week
10 pounds (4.5 kg)~10 weeks~5 weeks
20 pounds (9 kg)~20 weeks~10 weeks
50 pounds (23 kg)~50 weeks~25 weeks

So the honest summary: 10 pounds is a one to two month job, 20 pounds is a two to five month job, and 50 pounds is a six month to one year project. None of these are fast in the way the ads promise, but every one of them is completely doable. I lost about 90 pounds going from 128kg to 86kg, and the early months moved quicker than the later ones, exactly as the rate predicts.

Can you lose 10 pounds in a month?

Yes, but it is the aggressive end. 10 pounds in 4 weeks means about 2.5 pounds a week, which needs roughly an 850 calorie daily deficit. That is doable if you have a decent amount to lose and you are strict, but it is hard to keep up and a fair bit of an early fast drop is water, not fat. If you are already fairly lean, do not expect 10 pounds of fat in a month. Six to eight weeks is more honest, and the result will hold better.

My advice: aim for the steady rate, and treat any faster early loss as a bonus, not the expectation. People who chase the aggressive number and miss it feel like failures even when they are doing brilliantly. People who aim for the steady rate and beat it feel like winners. Same progress, completely different mindset.

How to actually hit the target

Pick your goal, find your timeline above, then build the deficit that gets you there:

1. Find your maintenance calories. Use the calculator on the homepage. It tells you how many calories you burn in a day.

2. Subtract your deficit. 500 a day for about 1 pound a week, up to 1,000 a day for about 2 pounds a week. Pick what you can actually live with.

3. Hit your protein. 1.6 to 2.2g per kg of goal weight so the weight you lose is fat, not muscle. See how much protein do I need.

4. Eat for volume so you can actually stick to it. The deficit on paper means nothing if you cannot hold it. Big, filling, low calorie meals are how. Read volume eating.

The fastest way to lose 50 pounds is to stop trying to lose it in a month. Pick a rate you can keep, and let the weeks do the work.

Set the goal, attach the realistic date, then ignore the daily scale noise and watch the weekly trend. If you want the full method behind these numbers, read the only diet that actually works, and if you are impatient, how to lose weight fast explains how to push the rate without wrecking it.
