The Only Diet That Actually Works

Diet Talk Fundamentals

I tried keto. Intermittent fasting. Paleo. Lost weight on all of them, then gained it back. Then I figured out why, and it changed everything.

Let me tell you something that took me years to figure out, and I am a bit annoyed nobody told me earlier.

Every popular diet works. Keto works. Intermittent fasting works. Paleo works. OMAD works. Low carb works. Low fat works. They all produce real results for real people.

Every popular diet also fails. The same diets. The same results reversed, six months later, by most of the same people.

I was one of those people. Multiple times. Hit 128kg, tried keto, lost 8kg, felt great for a month, then found myself at a family lunch with nothing to eat but plain salad while everyone else had nasi lemak. That lasted about three more weeks before I quietly gave up.

Every diet is the same diet underneath

Here is the thing nobody wants to say out loud because it ruins the marketing: every diet that causes weight loss does so through one mechanism. A calorie deficit. You are eating less energy than your body burns.

That is it. That is the whole thing.

Keto works because cutting carbs removes a huge chunk of the foods most people eat, so they end up eating less. Intermittent fasting works because squeezing your meals into a smaller window means you eat less overall. Paleo works because it cuts out processed food and most people eat less when they switch to whole foods.

None of them have a magic mechanism. None of them hack your hormones in some way that overrides physics. They are all just different strategies for putting you into a calorie deficit. The deficit is the thing. Everything else is packaging.

Before you say "but insulin" or "but fat burning mode", I know. Those mechanisms are real. But the weight loss always traces back to the calorie deficit. The research is extremely clear on this. People who eat the exact same calories lose the exact same amount of weight regardless of whether those calories came from mostly carbs or mostly fat.

Why this is harder than it sounds

Knowing that a calorie deficit causes weight loss does not automatically make it easy to be in one. If it were that simple, everyone would just eat a bit less and be fine. The reason diets fail is not ignorance of the calorie math. It is hunger.

When you eat less than your body needs, it produces hunger signals. Strong ones. Your body does not know you are trying to lose weight for the wedding in three months. It thinks you are starving. It responds accordingly.

Most people can tolerate hunger for a few days. Some can do a few weeks. Almost nobody can sustain it for months on willpower alone. At some point, the hunger wins. And then because you feel like you failed, you overcorrect, eat everything in sight, and end up heavier than when you started. Sound familiar?

This is why diets that restrict entire food groups are a trap. Keto bans rice. That works fine if you live in the UK. In Malaysia, rice is in everything, it is at every meal, it is how we eat socially, it is culturally embedded. The restriction is not just inconvenient, it is isolating. And that isolation is not sustainable for years.

The thing that separates people who keep the weight off

I have watched a lot of people lose weight over the years, including myself. The ones who kept it off permanently had one thing in common: they found a way of eating they could maintain without feeling deprived.

Not a diet. A way of eating. Indefinitely. For the rest of their lives.

That reframe matters a lot. A "diet" is temporary. Something you go on and come off. A way of eating is just how you eat. You do not quit it, because there is nothing to quit.

For me that meant not cutting rice. Not cutting chicken rice, or nasi lemak on weekends, or laksa at the kopitiam. What changed was how much I ate, not what. I learned my calorie target. I learned roughly how many calories were in the foods I actually eat. I started being honest about portions. That was the whole change.

Not glamorous. Not a brand name you can sell. But I went from 128kg down to 86kg, and these days I hold steady around 92kg without thinking about it. I eat rice every day. If you want the whole story, here is exactly how I lost the weight.

What to actually do

Step one is knowing your numbers. How many calories does your body burn in a day? That is your TDEE, Total Daily Energy Expenditure. To lose weight, you eat less than that number. To build muscle, you eat a bit more. Everything else flows from that.

Use the calculator on the homepage to get your exact number. It takes two minutes. Put in your age, weight, height, and how active you are. It spits out your maintenance calories and your deficit target.

Step two is protein. Eat 1.6g to 2.2g of protein per kg of your goal body weight, every day. Protein keeps you full, protects muscle when you are in a deficit, and takes more calories to digest than carbs or fat. It is the most important macro by far.

Step three is volume. Fill your plate with high-volume, low-calorie foods. Big portions of vegetables. Lots of lean protein. Moderate amounts of rice. This is what keeps hunger manageable on a deficit. You are eating large amounts of food, just not large amounts of calories.

That is the whole system. There is no step four. No supplements, no cheat day rules, no approved food lists. Just: know your numbers, hit your protein, eat mostly filling food. Do that consistently for a few months, and you will lose weight without feeling like you are on a diet.

The best diet is the one you can actually follow for two years without crying about it.

Any diet that achieves a calorie deficit while keeping you satisfied enough to maintain it long term is the right diet for you. That might look completely different from mine. The only wrong answer is one that makes you miserable, because miserable is not sustainable, and sustainable is the whole game.
