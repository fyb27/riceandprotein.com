What Is TDEE and How Do You Use It?

Diet Talk Fundamentals

TDEE is the single most useful number in weight loss, and most people have never heard of it. Here is what it means, how to find yours in 30 seconds, and how I used mine to lose 42kg without giving up rice.

Every diet that has ever worked for anyone, keto, fasting, low carb, whatever, worked for the same boring reason: the person ate fewer calories than they burned. TDEE is simply the "calories you burn" half of that equation. Once you know it, weight loss stops being guesswork and becomes arithmetic.

What TDEE actually means

TDEE stands for Total Daily Energy Expenditure. It is every calorie your body burns in 24 hours, from four sources:

ComponentWhat it isShare of TDEE
BMRCalories burned just staying alive: heart, brain, breathing~60-70%
Daily movementWalking, standing, fidgeting, housework (NEAT)~15-25%
ExerciseGym, running, football, badminton~5-15%
DigestionEnergy used to process food (TEF)~10%

Notice something? Exercise is the smallest slice for most people. Your body burns most of its calories just existing and moving around normally. This is why "I will just exercise more" fails as a weight loss plan: an hour of hard gym work burns maybe 300 to 400 calories, which is one teh tarik and a roti canai. You cannot outrun your fork. I tried for years.

How to find your TDEE

Use the free TDEE calculator on the homepage. It takes your age, height, weight and activity level, runs the Mifflin-St Jeor equation (the most accurate of the standard formulas), and gives you your number in about 30 seconds, in kg and cm, no unit conversion gymnastics required.

The one place people go wrong is the activity level. Be honest. "Moderately active" means you genuinely train or play sport most days of the week. Sitting in the office all day and going to the gym twice a week is "lightly active" at best. Almost everyone overestimates this setting, gets a TDEE that is 300 calories too generous, and then wonders why the scale is not moving.

For a rough feel of the numbers: a 36 year old guy, 92kg and 175cm with a desk job and a few gym sessions a week lands somewhere around 2,400 to 2,600 calories. A smaller, less active person might be at 1,800. This spread is exactly why copying someone else's diet plan does not work. Their number is not your number.

How to use the number

Your TDEE is your maintenance level. The number where you stay the same weight. Everything else follows from it:

To lose weight: eat around 500 calories below TDEE. That is roughly half a kilo of fat per week. If your TDEE is 2,500, you eat 2,000. That is not starvation territory. That is a normal day of food if you choose it deliberately: a decent breakfast, a proper lunch, dinner, done. When I started at 128kg my TDEE was over 3,000, which meant I could eat 2,500 calories a day, feel completely full, and still lose steadily.

To maintain: eat at TDEE. This is what I do now at 92kg. Maintenance is its own skill and nobody teaches it.

To gain muscle: eat 200 to 300 above TDEE while lifting. More than that mostly gains you fat, not muscle.

Two rules make the number work in practice. First, pair it with a protein target, around 1.6 to 2.2g per kg of goal weight. The calculator gives you this too, and here is exactly why it matters. Second, recalculate every 5kg or so. A lighter body burns less, so the deficit that worked at 110kg is quietly gone by 95kg. That is the famous "plateau". It is usually just an outdated TDEE.

What a TDEE budget looks like in real food

Numbers only click when you see them as actual meals. Say your target is 2,000 calories. Here is one way that day can go: a plain nasi lemak bungkus for breakfast (556), chicken breast with rice and vegetables for lunch (600), bak kut teh with lean cuts and a little rice for dinner (610), and you still have 230 left for fruit or a kopi. That is a genuinely good eating day, and it is a losing-weight day for most guys my size.

Now the same 2,000 spent badly: a char kuey teow (742), two teh tarik (260), a burger for dinner (600), some biscuits at the office (300), and you are at 1,900 having been hungry the entire day. Same budget, completely different experience. This is why I keep a whole database of Malaysian food calories on this site: once you know the prices, you shop smarter with the same money.

The three classic TDEE mistakes

Mistake one: treating the calculator number as gospel. It is a very good estimate, within about 10 percent for most people. Eat your target for two to three weeks, watch the scale trend (not the daily jumps, those are water), and adjust by 100 to 200 calories if nothing is happening. The calculator starts the process. The scale finishes it.

Mistake two: eating back exercise calories. Your activity level is already baked into the TDEE. If you told the calculator you train four times a week, those workouts are counted. Adding another 400 calories because your watch said you "earned" them is double counting, and it erases your deficit.

Mistake three: crash deficits. Eating 1,200 when your TDEE is 2,500 works for about two weeks, then hunger wins, then the weekend undoes everything, then you conclude "diets do not work for me". A moderate deficit you can hold for six months beats a brutal one you can hold for six days. Every single time.

Your TDEE is your budget. Weight loss is just spending less than you earn, with food you actually enjoy.

That is the whole concept. One number, honestly calculated, checked against the scale, spent on food you like. Work out your TDEE now, it takes 30 seconds, and if you want the full story of how this one number took me from 128kg to 86kg, that story is here.
